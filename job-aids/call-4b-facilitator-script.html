Global Settings & Final Review

Purpose: This is a full trainer/facilitator script for the final session in the onboarding arc: the three global settings areas (Account, Accounting, Lease), a handful of settings mechanics worth knowing cold (Chart of Accounts, role-based access, Pods, Payout Approved by Default), a final review of account setup, and closing out onboarding by introducing the customer's ongoing Account Manager. Use it to prepare for explaining these processes to a new team member, an owner, or yourself while practicing. Lines marked "Suggested trainer language" are ready-to-use scripting you can say almost word for word.

Introduction

Good morning, and welcome to the last session of onboarding. Today is really two things: a proper walkthrough of the three global settings areas that control how the entire system behaves, and then a final review before we hand you off to your ongoing Account Manager.

If we get through everything and you don't have any other questions, we're allowed to end this one early — there's no requirement to fill the full hour if you're in good shape.

The Three Settings Areas: Account, Accounting, Lease

Global settings break down into three areas, and it's worth framing each one by what it actually controls, rather than just its name.

  • Account Settings — your company information, branding, and the email preferences your tenants and owners actually see.
  • Accounting Settings — default bank accounts, management fee structures, and payment rules. This is the one we'll spend the most time on, because it directly controls how money flows through the entire system — which touches reporting, payouts, and reconciliation all at once.
  • Lease Settings — rent charges, late fee rules, proration behavior, and how a new lease activates.

Account Settings: Company Info, Branding, Email Preferences

Account Settings is where your company's identity lives inside the system: your company information, your branding — logo, colors, anything that shows up on tenant- and owner-facing documents — and your email preferences, which control the look and feel of what tenants and owners actually receive in their inbox.

Accounting Settings: Bank Accounts, Management Fees, Payment Rules

This is the section I want you to treat as the most important one in the entire settings area. It sets your default bank accounts, your management fee structures, and your payment rules, and because of that, it touches reporting, owner payouts, and bank reconciliation all at the same time. If something's off in here, it doesn't stay contained — it shows up everywhere downstream.

Accounting Settings isn't just one more settings page — it's the page that decides how money actually flows through your entire account. Take your time here.

Chart of Accounts: A Permanent Choice

One first-time-setup detail worth knowing before you touch anything: Chart of Accounts account types are permanent once created. You can't go back and change an account's type later, so it's worth getting right the first time rather than fixing it after the fact.

Role-Based Access and the View-Only Role

Role-based access controls who can see and do what inside the account, including a dedicated view-only role for anyone who needs visibility without the ability to make changes. Worth knowing: this applies equally to human users and to API keys — an API key is granted a role just like a person is, and it's bound by the same restrictions.

Pods: When (and When Not) to Use Portfolio Groups

Pods, also called portfolio groups, restrict staff access by grouping portfolios together. This is explicitly a large-company feature — it's built for accounts managing 1,000 or more units. If you're a smaller company, you don't need this, and it's worth saying so directly rather than letting someone feel like they're missing a feature they actually don't need yet.

Payout Approved by Default

Payout Approved by Default is a global accounting toggle that removes the manual approval step from owner payouts. This is generally recommended for accounts that aren't processing funds electronically through Rentvine — if you're not relying on Rentvine's own payment processing, this toggle saves you a step that isn't adding any real protection for your account.

Lease Settings: Rent Charges, Late Fees, Proration

Lease Settings governs how rent charges post, how late fees are calculated and applied, how proration works for a lease that starts or ends mid-month, and how a new lease activates once it's created. Getting these right up front means you won't be second-guessing individual lease behavior later.

Final Review Checklist

Before we close out, let's run through a final review of your account setup together:

  • Management fee settings — confirmed and matching what was agreed upon.
  • Late fee settings — confirmed for how your company actually wants to charge them.
  • Payout information — confirmed and ready to actually pay owners out correctly.

If anything looks off in any of these three, now's the time to fix it — not after you're fully live and running real transactions through them.

Introducing the Account Manager and Closing Out Onboarding

Once the final review is done, this session closes out the entire onboarding arc. From here, the training team hands you off to your ongoing Account Manager, who becomes your go-to point of contact going forward.

You'll get an introduction to that Account Manager, along with a graduation or completion email marking the end of onboarding.

You've made it through the full onboarding program. From here, your Account Manager is going to be your main point of contact — we're introducing you to them today, and you'll get a completion email confirming you're fully onboarded.

And again — if there aren't any further questions at this point, it's completely fine to end the call early. There's no requirement to stretch this out.

Closing

That's the full final session: the three global settings areas, the mechanics worth knowing in each one, a final review of your account setup, and the handoff to your Account Manager to close out onboarding.
