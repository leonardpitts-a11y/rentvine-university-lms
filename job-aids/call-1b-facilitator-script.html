Properties, Associations, Marketing & Move-In

Purpose: This is a full trainer/facilitator script covering the Properties tab (details, images, rental details, lease and portfolio details, rent finder reports, documents, appliances/filters, associations, custom fields, property groups, and tags), marketing and syndication, the move-in/lease creation flow, and personal and account notification settings. Use it to prepare for explaining these processes to a new team member, an owner, or yourself while practicing. Lines marked "Suggested trainer language" are ready-to-use scripting you can say almost word for word.

Introduction

Let's go ahead and get started. Today's session is going to cover everything inside the Properties tab, including associations and marketing. Once we finish that, we'll move on to the move-in flow: how to create a lease and move in a tenant. Lastly, we'll finish up with personal and account notification settings.

So first things first: the Properties tab.

Properties Tab Overview

If you click on the Property tab on the left-hand side, you're going to see three drop-downs: Properties, Associations, and Marketing. The first one we're going to talk about is Properties.

Once you click into Properties, it's similar to Portfolios: you'll see a list of all the different properties in your system, plus a search bar. You can quickly type in the property you're looking for and it will take you into the details page of that property.

Once you get into the details page, we're going to talk about a few major items in depth, and then the rest will just be a broad overview.

Property Details: Basics and Images

The first section is Details. Here you'll see name, address, property type, legal description, year built, and county.

A major important part here is images. You'll be able to see the different images that are attached to the property overall. If you want to add photos, you can click the Add Photos icon, or just drag and drop from your computer.

If you click on any of the images, you'll see the photo selector. You can scroll or click between every single image, and you have the ability to select one as the main photo — just click on it, and you can always change it back later.

If you want to reorganize how the photos/images look, you can hold down an image and drag it to the correct location.

Rental Details, Lease Details, and Portfolio Details

The next section is Rental Details. This is part of the marketing of the property overall. It is not a requirement, but it's strongly recommended that you have this information pre-filled out, so that when you actually get to the marketing portion, you don't have to enter the information again.

The next section is Lease Details. Here you can see which tenant is currently living at the property. You can tell whether there's a current lease, a future lease, or past leases, and how many past leases there are. If you click View All, you'll see every separate tenant who has been in this lease.

Outside of Lease Details, you'll also have Portfolio Details, where you can see who the actual owners of the property are, which portfolio they belong to, and the actual owners who own the majority of the property overall.

Rent Finders: Optional Market Rent Reports

Moving forward, we have Rent Finders. This is completely optional. If you choose to run a Rent Finder report, you can do so by clicking the grayed-out button at the top of the screen.

You'll be able to see the cost to run the rent-finder report, and basically it will be a report about what similar units are being rented for in the market. This is done through AI and does a quick scan of what's happening in the area.

If you wish to see a sample report, you can click View Sample and it will take you to a copy of a randomized report so you can see what it looks like.

Documents at the Property Level

The next section is Documents. Similar to the conversation from a previous session, in case you sent any electronic documents from the property section, they'll be attached to this Documents tab, and they'll always live here at the property level. Depending on the type you select, that's where the document is going to sit.

Appliances and Filters

The next section is Appliances and Filters. If you want to add appliances to the property, you can, and the same goes for filters. All you have to do is find the actual filter size, or select a custom one and enter a description for the filter. This is typically used if you have a resident benefits package.

Homeowners Associations

The next section is Homeowners Association, where you have the ability to add an association, and those get treated as a vendor.

In case you're wondering where to add the association, you'll have the Associations tab right underneath Properties, where you can simply add a new one. Click the green button in the top-right corner and proceed with entering the details overall.

Custom Fields, Property Groups, and Tags

If we go back to the property, we'll also start seeing custom fields. A dead giveaway is that you'll see two little dashes on the screen — that tells you it's not a default field, it's actually a custom field, and you have the ability to customize it however you prefer.

The different custom field categories include single line text, paragraph, drop-down, toggle, date, numeric, currency, and whole number.

Outside of custom fields, you also have the ability to add property groups as well as tags. You already know what tags are, so the only thing to talk about here is property groups.

If you ever need to create a group — for example, properties that belong to a specific county, or that are in the northwest, east, or south side of the city — you can do so. Or, if you want to group properties by specific owner contacts as far as payments (for example, if you have owners who prefer to be paid out on the 10th), you can create property groups for that and include those groups.

Marketing the Property: Syndication Setup

Now talking about the Details tab, the next section is Marketing: how do we want to proceed with marketing the property overall?

On the Marketing tab itself, you'll have several different sections of information that need to be filled out. There are five required items, and whenever you go through the process, they will be required: syndication, property description, contact information, rental details, and property features.

Outside of those, you'll have to manually enter information if you want it included in what gets sent out for syndication: utilities, restrictions, parking, area information, property details, and lease availability.

Once you're ready to put this property out for marketing, simply click the toggle. Once you click the toggle, the system will already have information pre-filled for you, and it's a five-step process, so you just proceed through the flow:

  • Enter the contact information.
  • Select what property features the specific area has.
  • Enter the actual listing details.
  • Provide your listing description.
  • Select directly what you are syndicating this out to — your website, syndication providers, or specific networks.

Once you're set, all you have to do is click Activate, and now the property is actually out for marketing.

Activating and Reviewing Active Marketing

Once you activate a listing, if you want a list of all the marketing properties you currently have active, you can click the Marketing tab on the left-hand side and see every single one of them that's active.

Bills, Maintenance, and Inspections at the Property Level

Going back to the property overall, besides the Marketing section, you have the ability to see Bills. Similar to Portfolios, these are just filtered by the property directly.

You also have Maintenance, similarly filtered directly by the property.

Then you have Inspections. Inspections come over from our system directly — if you're doing any inspections through Rentvine, you'll find them under the Maintenance section. If you have a syndication partner like RentCheck or SecureInspect, any of those inspections will get added into the Files tab of the property instead, so you will not see them in the Inspections section.

One thing to be aware of: once you create an inspection, you can no longer delete it. It will stay on the property, so be careful if you're testing the system and doing dummy inspections — you will not be able to get rid of them.

Files, Budgets, and Accounting Settings

Next, you also have the ability to have Files, as well as Budgets if you choose to create one.

The last thing to talk about on the property is Settings. There are two major items to talk about here: Accounting Settings and Management Settings.

Under Accounting Settings, this is where you're able to set an actual reserve amount that gets added at the property level. Besides the reserve, you also have the ability to add a markup. If you do markups directly for your owners and want to set it up at the actual property level, you can put the markup at the property level, and every time you have a bill for this property, a markup will be added.

Outside of markups, if you're in the state of California, you'll have the withholding tax agency field. And if your state uses sales tax, you can also configure that as well.

Management Settings and Fee Structure Timing

The next section is Management Settings. The important item to note is your management fee structure — you'll be able to see what you're actually collecting for management fees. Management fees are set at the property level, not the unit level, so remember that when setting up specific structures.

The only thing to point out about management fee settings is that if you make changes in the middle of the actual month period and you already have charges that were posted and unpaid, the system is not going to backtrack. You'll need to proceed with avoiding the charge and recreating it once it's set, or — what's highly recommended — wait until the next cycle.

Right before rent is posted, once everybody has already paid, go ahead and proceed with actually changing the management fee structure so you can collect the correct amounts.

Other Property Settings Cards

Besides Management Settings, you'll have other cards with information: property insurance, maintenance settings, marketing settings, and screening settings.

That was basically the details of the actual property overall — the same generic items you'd want to review inside a property, plus how to market the property. The next step is the actual move-in flow.

Move-In Flow: Two Ways to Create a Lease

There are two ways for you to process a move-in. It's either going to be from the application, if you're actually screening a customer through our system — in that case, you can create a lease directly from that same application. Or, if not, you can manually create the lease from the property directly.

For this example, we're going to proceed with creating it from scratch. Once you're on the details page of the property, you can click Create Lease and start the move-in flow directly.

Move-In Flow: Lease Terms and Fees

From the move-in flow, you have the ability to start entering details: when exactly is the lease going to start, and how long is the term? For this example, the lease starts July 1st for one year.

I'm going to leave the late fee settings at the default: 25 initial and 10 daily. Same thing for the NSF fee amount.

I do not have an assignee. Remember, the assignee field is based on who you want notified when actions happen on the lease. If you're apartment-based rather than agent-based, you typically don't need an assignee entered.

The next thing to click is Next, which takes you to the Tenant step.

Move-In Flow: Adding Tenants

One thing to note: if you're doing this from the actual application, the information is already pre-filled, so you don't have to bring it over every time. If you don't run the application through Rentvine and don't see an application in the system, you'll need to manually enter this information.

A tenant is required in order to proceed, so make sure you're actually adding one. Click the gray Actions button, add a new tenant, and proceed with entering the information. Once you have at least one tenant added, you can proceed with entering cosigners, occupants, animals, emergency contacts, vehicles, tenant liability insurance, or renters insurance — or you can leave those until after the lease is created.

Once you have at least one tenant, click Next.

Move-In Flow: Monthly and Moving Charges

The next portion is Moving Charges. You'll have the ability to set up your monthly charges (the actual recurring charges, such as rent income and pet rent — remove pet rent if the tenant doesn't have pets) and your moving charges.

You'll also have the ability to set up escalations. In case you have multi-year leases, you can set up a rent increase to start at a specific date, with a specific dollar or percentage increase. You can add multiple escalations if needed.

Outside of monthly charges, you have advanced recurring charges — charges you're adding on a different frequency and start date than the standard monthly charge. For example, an AC filter charge could be set to recur every 3 months starting on a specific date.

Next is Moving Charges, which are your one-time charges: security deposit, first month's rent, and any one-time moving fees. You can add or delete these charges as needed, and add any additional one-time charges if needed.

Move-In Flow: Default Monthly and Move-In Charge Settings

One thing to mention about monthly and moving charges: you have the ability to set these up as defaults directly from your global settings. If you go to your initials in the bottom-left corner and select Settings, then scroll down to Lease, you have the ability to add default monthly charges and default move-in charges.

If you always want something to populate every single time you create a new lease, you can set that up there so it automatically generates for the tenant.

Move-In Flow: Review and Submitting the Lease

After entering charges, click Confirm, which takes you to the last page: the Review section. This tells you the details of the property, the tenants moving in, your monthly charges, your moving charges, and on the right side, some new options.

If you want to send a portal invitation, you can toggle that on. Outside of the portal invitation is lease activation: you can either create the lease as pending, activate the lease now, or automatically activate upon the start date of the lease. It's completely up to you.

One thing to advise: if you currently have somebody living at the property and you have not moved them out or changed the actual lease status, you will not be able to activate the new lease. You can also deactivate the listing — remember, if the property is still out for marketing when you're going through lease creation, you'll have the ability to deactivate that listing here.

You also have the ability to charge a lease setup fee to the owner if applicable — completely up to you what you decide to do.

Once everything is done, click Submit, and now you've created a brand-new financial lease in the system.

Managing an Active Lease: Dates, Agreements, Portal Invites, and Deletion Rules

You'll see that the deposit has not been paid yet. If you want to make adjustments to your move-in dates, you can — simply click there and change the dates.

If you want to generate a lease agreement from the new information at this point, you can do so, as well as send a portal invitation directly to your tenants if you haven't done so yet.

One thing to note: in case you have a financial transaction attached to the lease, you will not be able to delete it. That's exactly why, in this training example, charges were removed directly from the system before proceeding — otherwise the lease could not have been deleted.

Personal Notification Settings

The next thing we're going to talk about is personal and account notification settings. If you click on your initials in the bottom-left corner, you can select Settings. From there, you have the ability to go to either your Personal or your Account notifications.

Personal notifications are always set by the individual user — you have no control over what notifications someone else sets up for themselves; it's always what they set up for themselves.

You have the ability to come in here, enter your phone number, and connect your account to Slack if you have a Slack account. Then you can select what you want to be notified for — for example, whenever a tenant makes a payment to the portal, do you want to receive a notification? You can select in-system notification, email notification, text notification, and/or Slack notification.

The other thing to note is when you receive the notification. This is what the assignee field is for: if you're assigned to the lease, you'll receive the notification; if you're unassigned and the lease is assigned to you, you also receive it; or you can choose to only be notified for leases assigned specifically to you. So you get to decide when you're actually notified.

Account (Company-Wide) Notification Settings

Going back, if you go to Account Notifications, it's the same idea, but it's for the whole account — there's no assignee field. For example, if you're organized by department and want every maintenance coordinator to get notified whenever a work order is submitted by a resident, you can go to the Work Order section, add an email, and enter that email address as well as a phone number.

Once entered, that address will receive every one of those work-order-submitted notifications, and you can also choose to receive the phone number notification. So you have the ability to select exactly who receives those notifications, but this is not based on assignee — it's based on the whole account, so you don't get to filter it the same way you can with personal notifications.

Besides manager notifications, you'll also have owner notifications, technician notifications, applicant notifications, and tenant notifications. You'll be able to see what's set up by default and what other notifications you can pursue setting up for the other parties involved.

Closing

That's pretty much the training for today. We went over the property overview — the actual details and settings in depth — talked about marketing properties and how to look into your active marketing, and then created a lease from the move-in flow.
