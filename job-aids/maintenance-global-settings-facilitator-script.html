Introduction

This call was a global-settings walkthrough, not a work-order-creation walkthrough — the customer (a third-party-vendor-only shop transitioning between inspection tools) was setting up the maintenance module from the ground up before go-live. That settings-first framing is why several of the details below live one level "above" the work order lifecycle RU already teaches: they're configuration decisions made once, in Settings > Maintenance, that shape how every future work order, inspection, and vendor interaction behaves.

Maintenance Categories: Filtering Vendor Selection

Maintenance categories aren't just a reporting label — they're assigned directly to vendor contacts, and Rentvine uses that assignment to filter which vendors show up as options when a work order is being created. If a vendor is tagged as "Plumbing," they'll surface as a suggested match when a work order's category is plumbing-related, instead of a manager scrolling the full vendor list every time.

This is separate from vendor types, which exist mainly for reporting rather than filtering.

Set up your maintenance categories before you load in your vendors, then assign each vendor to the categories they actually work in. Once that's done, the vendor list on a new work order narrows itself down instead of you hunting through everyone.

Internal Maintenance Settings for Vendor-Only Shops

A useful trainer moment from this call: even a management company that exclusively uses third-party vendors (no in-house maintenance techs) still has an Internal Maintenance settings section, and it's worth explaining why it exists rather than assuming it's irrelevant to a vendor-only shop.

The internal maintenance settings include:

  • A default internal maintenance company name.
  • Labor descriptions and labor type names (used on invoices generated for internal techs).
  • An option to print total hours worked on invoices.
  • Material and labor cost tracking, tied to the internal price book.
  • A permission toggle for whether internal technicians can accept or reject work orders themselves.
  • A "no markup on material pricing" option for internal techs.

The trainer framing worth reusing: a management company typically collects payment from the owner/tenant side and then pays its own internal technicians separately, so these settings exist to support that internal payroll-adjacent workflow — not to replace the vendor relationship a company already has.

Even if you only work with outside vendors today, walk through the internal maintenance settings once so you know they're there. If you ever bring on an in-house tech, the price book and labor settings are already waiting for you instead of being a surprise later.

Global Default Inspection Areas, Items, and Actions

RU already covers how to perform a property inspection (marking items good/bad, adding photos, sharing the report). This call surfaced the settings-level piece that sits above that: default inspection areas, items, and actions are configured globally, in Settings, not per unit or per property.

That means when a manager sets up "Kitchen" as an area with items like "Cabinets," "Countertops," and "Appliances," those defaults apply system-wide to every inspection created afterward — they aren't something a user configures fresh on every single inspection. A company transitioning from a dedicated legacy inspection tool should expect to do this setup once, up front, rather than per property.

Build your inspection areas and items once, globally, before you start running inspections. You're not customizing this per property — you're setting the template every inspection will pull from.

Work Order Projects: Grouping Tasks for Turnovers

Work Order Projects group multiple individual work orders under one umbrella — commonly used for a property turnover or a move-in where several separate repairs and make-ready tasks need to happen together. The Projects view rolls up total estimated costs, owner bills, actual costs, profit margin, and completion percentage across every work order in the group, giving a manager a single place to track a turnover instead of checking each work order individually.

If you've got five or six things to fix before a unit is ready to re-rent, don't create five separate untracked work orders — group them into a project so you can see total cost and completion percentage for the whole turnover in one place.

Inviting a Vendor to the Vendor Portal

RU already covers what a vendor sees and does once they're in their portal (accepting/rejecting work orders, scheduling, submitting invoices). This call added the admin-side first step: to actually get a vendor into their portal in the first place, go to the vendor's contact record, scroll to Portal Access/Invitation, and send the portal invite.

Worth pairing with the existing note elsewhere in RU that a vendor who tries to register with an email already tied to another account will hit a login error — if a vendor reports they can't accept the invite, check whether that email is already registered before troubleshooting further.

AI-Assisted Vendor Invoice Reading

When a vendor uploads an invoice, Rentvine's AI automatically reads the document and auto-fills roughly 70 percent of the invoice data (line items, amounts, vendor details) rather than requiring a manager to key it all in by hand. It's a genuine time-saver, but set the right expectation: it's a head start, not a guarantee — staff should still glance over the auto-filled fields before converting the invoice into a bill.

A related question that came up but wasn't fully resolved on the call: whether a vendor's payment terms (for example, net-30) can drive the invoice's due date automatically through the discount/grace-period settings. That's worth testing before promising it to a customer rather than stating it as confirmed behavior.

When a vendor uploads their invoice, Rentvine's AI will read it and pre-fill most of the fields for you — usually around 70 percent of the data. Still give it a quick look before you convert it to a bill, since it's a head start, not the final word.

Work Order Note and File Sharing Defaults

Two visibility defaults worth calling out explicitly, since customers often assume the opposite: notes added to a work order are not automatically shared with anyone — sharing a note with the owner, tenant, or vendor is a manual, case-by-case decision made when the note is created or updated. Likewise, general file uploads on a work order do not automatically become visible to tenants or owners just because they're attached.

Adding a note or a file to a work order doesn't automatically share it with your owner or tenant — you choose who sees it, note by note and file by file. If you want them to see something, you have to share it on purpose.

In-App Messaging Limitations

Rentvine's built-in text messaging is intentionally basic: there's no date filtering on message history and no read receipts, and it requires logging into the system to check. For anything that needs a documented, reliable confirmation trail, recommend following up by email instead of relying on a text thread alone — at least until a broader global communications feature (mentioned as in development, not yet released) rolls out.

Texting through Rentvine works fine for quick back-and-forth, but it doesn't have read receipts or date filtering, so don't rely on it alone for anything you need a paper trail on. Follow up important confirmations by email until the bigger communications update ships.

Submitting a Feature Request

When a customer asks for functionality Rentvine doesn't currently have, the process is to email support@rentvine.com with as much detail and context as possible — the more specific the request, the easier it is for the development team to evaluate and scope. Encourage customers to describe the actual workflow problem they're trying to solve, not just the feature name, so it's clear why it matters.

Maintenance on the Mobile App: Current State and Roadmap

Today, the Rentvine mobile app is built for property managers, not for vendors or internal technicians — internal technicians access their work orders through the vendor portal (in a browser) rather than a dedicated app. Maintenance and inspection functionality for the mobile app is currently in development, so this is a "not yet, but coming" answer rather than a hard no if a customer asks about a technician-facing mobile experience.

Property-Level Alerts for Owner Restrictions

A property-level alert can be configured to pop up automatically whenever a work order is created for that property — useful for flagging things like an owner's pre-approval requirement or a repeat-issue restriction (for example, a tenant who has called in the same complaint multiple times). It's a lightweight way to make sure a restriction the office already knows about doesn't get missed by whoever happens to create the next work order.

If an owner always wants a heads-up before any repair over a certain amount, don't rely on everyone remembering that — set a property-level alert so it pops up automatically the next time a work order gets created there.

Price Book Markups: A Concrete Example

A simple worked example is useful for explaining how the internal price book's purchase-price-vs-owner-price fields translate into a markup: a material like paper might have a $5 purchase price (what the company actually pays) and a $15 owner price (what gets billed to the owner) — the $10 difference is the markup the management company keeps. Use a concrete pair of numbers like this rather than describing the fields abstractly; it makes the markup concept click faster.

When a Customer Asks About a Third-Party Invoicing Tool

More than one customer has asked whether a third-party maintenance/invoicing tool they already use (Venderoo and Iron Ledger have both come up) integrates directly with Rentvine. Neither has a confirmed native integration today. The consistent, honest answer: Rentvine's API is open, so a direct integration is technically possible, but nothing prebuilt exists for these specific tools right now — encourage the customer to check with that vendor's support team about what they currently support, and note the request internally if it's a genuine feature gap.

I'm not familiar with that specific tool's integration status, but Rentvine's API is open, so it's technically possible. I'd check with their support team on what they currently connect to, and if it's something you need, let's get it logged as a feature request too.

Confirming In-Progress Work Orders During Migration

Open work orders do migrate into Rentvine as part of a standard data migration. But for work orders that are mid-progress at the moment of cutover, don't assume every detail (scheduling state, partial estimates, vendor acceptance status) carries over cleanly — the customer should confirm the specifics of in-progress work order migration directly with their onboarding specialist rather than assuming full parity.
