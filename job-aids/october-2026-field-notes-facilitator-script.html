Off-Portal Payments: Zelle, Venmo & the Bank-Run Pattern

The core rule trainees already know: any payment that arrives outside the tenant portal needs a manual receipt and a manual deposit. Two October refinements are worth adding.

Zelle and Venmo are always manual

On the Barron Q/A call (Oct 6, 2026), a customer asked whether Zelle rent payments could be automated. The answer is no: Zelle can't be connected to or automated inside Rentvine. Money that lands in any bank account by Zelle or Venmo is treated exactly like a paper check — record the receipt on the lease, then record the deposit, dated to match when the funds actually hit the bank.

If it didn't come through the portal, Rentvine has no idea it exists. Zelle, Venmo, a money order, a housing authority check — they're all the same two steps: receipt, then deposit, dated the day the bank actually saw the money.

The bank-run consolidation pattern

Also from Barron (Oct 6): when several checks go to the bank as one physical deposit, the Rentvine deposit should mirror that — one consolidated deposit that checks off all the included receipts against the trust account. If someone already recorded a premature single-check deposit, void it first, then build the consolidated one. The deposit date must match the real bank transaction date, and stay in the same month — a mismatch here is one of the most common reasons a reconciliation breaks across month boundaries (reinforced on the CornerStone 2A call, Oct 7).

Your deposit in Rentvine should look exactly like the deposit slip the bank saw: same checks, same total, same date. One trip to the bank, one deposit record.

Why We Avoid Bank Adjustments

On the CornerStone 2A call (Oct 7, 2026), the trainer's guidance was unambiguous: avoid the bank adjustment feature. Adjustments put a raw plus-or-minus on the bank ledger without the property, lease, and GL context that the rest of the accounting engine expects, and in practice they cause reconciliation imbalances that are painful to unwind later. The recommended alternative is to use an "other receipt" (money in) or an "other payment" (money out) tied to the right property and GL account, which keeps diagnostics and owner statements coherent.

If you're reaching for a bank adjustment, stop and ask what actually happened to the money. There's almost always an other receipt or other payment that records the same dollars with the context reconciliation needs.

The Unprinted Checks Queue

From Welcome Home (Oct 5) and CornerStone 2A (Oct 7): every payment recorded as a check increments an "unprinted checks" counter on the banking tab. Companies that actually print checks from Rentvine should treat that counter as a work queue and clear it with each print run. Companies that hand-write checks or pay another way should record those payments as "other" payment types instead, so phantom unprinted checks don't pile up. Related housekeeping: the starting check number is editable in bank account settings, so a customer migrating mid-checkbook can pick up exactly where their physical checks left off.

That unprinted checks number should be boring — either it's a print queue you clear every run, or it stays at zero because you record non-printed payments as "other." If it's quietly climbing, something's being recorded wrong.

The Month-to-Month Premium Toggle

Confirmed across CornerStone 1B (Oct 6), Evolve PMG 1B (Oct 6), and Doug Shorter PM 1B (Oct 7): lease setup includes a month-to-month premium toggle. When a fixed-term lease reaches its end date and rolls month-to-month, the premium automatically raises the rent charge by the configured amount — no manual re-charging needed. This pairs naturally with the renewal conversation in Week 16: the premium is both a revenue protection and a nudge that encourages tenants to sign a renewal rather than drift month-to-month.

Set the month-to-month premium when you build the lease, not when the lease expires. Future-you will forget; the toggle won't.

Applicant Workarounds: No SSN, Passports & Saved Searches

Applicants without an SSN

From Welcome Home (Oct 5, 2026): when an applicant has no Social Security number, the accepted workaround is entering all nines in the SSN field to move the application through, and a passport is a valid identity document for the file. Screening results will be limited without an SSN, so the decision still needs human review — but the application itself doesn't have to stall.

Keeping the applications screen usable

From Edwards (Oct 6, 2026): the applications screen lists every submission chronologically, including applicants who already signed a lease. The cleanup move is a saved search filtered on "has lease = no," which leaves only applicants still in play. The same call surfaced that Rentvine has no built-in application-verification checklist — the practical workaround is pasting the company's standard verification checklist into the application's notes so every processor works the same list.

Your applications screen should show you decisions you still owe someone — not a history lesson. Save the "has lease = no" search once and make it the default way your team opens that screen.

Two Report-Filter Diagnostics Worth Teaching

Finding leases with no recurring charges

From Welcome Home (Oct 5, 2026): a lease that never got its recurring rent charge is silent revenue loss — nothing posts, nothing shows past due. The reliable way to find these is filtering the leases report on recurring charge count using the "is empty" operator. The intuitive filter — "equals zero" — does not catch them, because a lease with no recurring charge rows has no value to equal zero. This distinction between empty and zero is worth stating explicitly; it trips up almost everyone once.

"Equals zero" asks every lease what its count is — and a lease with no charges at all never answers. "Is empty" is the filter that finds the silent ones. Run it monthly.

A saved "unpaid rent" delinquency report

Same call: build a monthly delinquency view from the lease charges report — amount due at least $1, charge date filtered to the current rent cycle (for example, October 1), "paid in full" set to no, grouped by account — and save it. The reason for the tight date filter: running it against all time pollutes the view with legacy migration noise and makes the paid percentage meaningless.

Known Bugs & Gaps: Setting Expectations

These came up live during the first week of October 2026. Teach them so trainees can set customer expectations honestly rather than promising behavior the system doesn't have. Statuses were accurate as of the calls referenced — verify against current release notes before repeating them as still-open.

  • Saved cards despite disabled card payments (Edwards, Oct 6): tenants can still save credit cards to their portal wallet even when card payments are disabled on the lease. The card can't be used to pay, but its presence confuses tenants. Acknowledged on the call as behavior that should be restricted.
  • Bulk tenant receipt auto-allocation bug (CornerStone 2A, Oct 7): bulk tenant receipts work for recording one payment across many leases (the Section 8 pattern from Week 16), but a known bug currently prevents the bulk amount from auto-allocating to unpaid charges — allocation needs manual verification until fixed.
  • No outbound SMS log (Edwards, Oct 6): there is no backend log of outgoing text messages. In the real case that surfaced this, a deactivated vendor's phone kept receiving work order texts a week after deactivation, and proving it required the vendor's own screenshots. Escalations about stray texts need customer-side evidence.
  • No dashboard of automated tenant emails (Edwards, Oct 6): there's no internal screen showing which automated system emails (like past-due notices) went to which tenant. You can view default templates and customize lease email templates, but delivery history isn't surfaced.
  • Pinned dashboard filters don't persist (Welcome Home, Oct 5; Edwards, Oct 6): pinned filters reset on navigation or refresh. Saved searches are the durable alternative — teach those instead.
  • Physical lockbox codes don't integrate (CornerStone 1B, Oct 6): code boxes on doors have no integration; codes are updated manually in property details.
When a customer hits one of these, the worst answer is pretending it's user error. Name it as a known limitation, show the workaround, and they'll trust everything else you say more.

Real Case: The Portfolio Transfer That Went Sideways

From the Edwards Property Management call (Oct 6, 2026) — a concrete example of why portfolio transfers deserve respect. Edwards had moved four to five properties between portfolios. Afterward, a $3,810 security deposit was sitting on the wrong lease (a yard-space lease instead of the residential unit), one portfolio showed a negative operating balance of about $540, and escrow was misaligned by roughly $19,320. The resolution path on the call: transfer the affected properties back, then verify the old portfolio zeroed out before attempting anything further.

The teaching points: a portfolio change migrates unpaid charges, prepayments, credits, security deposits, bills, and work orders automatically — and the transfer is permanent once confirmed. That automation is powerful when the target is right and expensive when it's wrong. Before any transfer, snapshot the source portfolio's balances; after it, confirm the source zeroed out and the deposits landed on the correct leases.

A portfolio transfer moves everything, permanently, in one click. So the discipline is all before and after: know the balances going in, prove the old portfolio reads zero coming out, and check where the security deposits landed — not whether they moved, but whether they moved to the right lease.
